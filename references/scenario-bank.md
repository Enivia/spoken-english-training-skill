# Scenario Bank

Use one prompt at a time. Prefer a topic connected to information the learner already volunteered.

## `5+1` daily prompts

| Topic | Beginner prompt | Stretch prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | What did you eat today? Did you like it? | What made the meal memorable or disappointing? |
| Something noticed | What did you see on your way today? | Why did it catch your attention? |
| Content | Did you watch or read anything interesting? | How would you recommend or criticize it? |
| Work or study | What was difficult today? | What did you try, and what would you change next time? |
| Mood | How did you feel today? Why? | Did your mood change during the day? What caused it? |
| Reflection | What did you learn or wish you had done differently? | If you could repeat one moment, what would you do and why? |

For a beginner, optionally use one frame:

```text
Today I felt ___ because ___.
It reminded me of ___.
Tomorrow I will ___.
```

Move beyond the frame once the learner can produce connected sentences without it.

## Small-talk scenes

Choose the closest adjacent scene to the learner's story.

| Scene | Natural opening | Useful follow-up direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classmate | How's your day going? | class, assignment, lunch, weekend |
| Coworker | Has it been a busy day for you too? | workload, commute, plans after work |
| Café or shop | How's your day been so far? | popular item, weather, neighborhood |
| Rideshare | Has it been busy today? | traffic, local events, driving hours |
| Neighbor or elevator | Nice weather today, isn't it? | building, pets, nearby places |
| Event or party | How do you know the host? | food, venue, shared interests |

Treat these as seeds, not scripts. Match the other person's energy and keep questions easy to answer.

## Run a role-play

1. State the setting and roles in one short line.
2. Open naturally as the other person.
3. Respond to what the learner actually says.
4. Ask no more than one question per turn.
5. Keep the coach's dialogue to one or two sentences.
6. After 3–5 turns, pause for the feedback rubric and then resume or close.

For a stalled learner, offer one repair phrase instead of writing their complete reply:

- `Let me think for a second.`
- `How should I put it?`
- `What I mean is...`
- `I don't know the exact word, but...`

## Build a small-talk response

Teach this portable pattern when answers are too short:

```text
answer + one detail + a return question
```

Example:

```text
Yeah, it's my first time here. A friend recommended the place. Do you come here often?
```
